The Quran's account of how ʿĀd came to an end is brief, and vivid. They demanded the punishment they had been warned of. And then, the text says,

**[5.03]** _Quranic text_

Elsewhere the wind is called ṣarṣar, raging, or roaring, and al-ʿaqīm, the barren wind. It blew, the Quran says, "for seven long nights and eight long days". Hūd, and those who believed with him, were saved.

**[5.04]** _Authentic hadith_ · on-screen: `HADITH_AISHA`

The memory of that wind stayed with the Prophet Muhammad. His wife ʿĀʾisha reported that whenever he saw clouds or wind, concern showed on his face. When she asked him why, he said that a people had been punished with wind, and that when they saw it coming, they had said, "This cloud will give us rain."

**[5.05]** _Authentic hadith_ · on-screen: `HADITH_SABA`

In another authentic report, he said: "I was granted victory with the east wind, and ʿĀd were destroyed by the west wind."

**[5.06]** _Scientific or geographical evidence / hypothesis_ · on-screen: `SCIENCE_DIVIDER`

Science cannot test a religious account of divine judgement. But it can ask a different question: how has this land itself changed?

**[5.07]** _Scientific or geographical evidence / hypothesis_

In a cave called Qunf, in the mountains of Dhofar, a stalagmite has recorded rainfall for thousands of years, layer by layer. A study published in two thousand and three found that monsoon rain here was stronger in the early Holocene, and that after about eight thousand years ago, it gradually declined.

**[5.08]** _Scientific or geographical evidence / hypothesis_

Deep in the Rubʿ al-Khālī, researchers reported in two thousand and twenty-five that, during an ancient humid phase, heavy rains filled a lake of about eleven hundred square kilometres, which eventually broke its banks and carved a valley some one hundred and fifty kilometres long.

**[5.09]** _Scientific or geographical evidence / hypothesis_

Arabia, in other words, was not always this dry. Over many thousands of years, its landscapes have shifted between green and gold.

**[5.10]** _Narrative framing_

But we should be clear. These changes unfolded over centuries and millennia. No study has dated the people of ʿĀd, and no geological event has been shown to match the Quranic account. At Shisr, the excavators found no evidence that a sandstorm destroyed the site. The collapse of its fortress into a sinkhole has a geological explanation, and no known connection to ʿĀd.

**[5.11]** _Narrative framing_

Religious memory and physical evidence are answering different questions. Merging them would do justice to neither.
