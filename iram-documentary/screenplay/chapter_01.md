# THE LOST CITY OF IRAM

## Chapter 1 — The Disappearance

**[1.01]** _Scientific or geographical evidence / hypothesis_

The Rubʿ al-Khālī, the Empty Quarter, covers some six hundred and fifty thousand square kilometres of the Arabian Peninsula, across parts of Saudi Arabia, Oman, the United Arab Emirates and Yemen. It is often described as the largest continuous sand desert on Earth. In places, its dunes rise more than two hundred metres.

**[1.02]** _Later tradition / legend_

For centuries, those who lived along its edges told stories of a people of great strength, of towering buildings, and of a city that was lost.

**[1.03]** _Quranic text_ · on-screen: `Q89_6_8`

The oldest written source for that memory is the Quran. In a short passage of Sūrat al-Fajr, the chapter of the Dawn, it asks:

**[1.04]** _Quranic text_ · on-screen: `Q89_6_8`

"Dost thou not consider how thy Lord dealt with the tribe of ʿĀd, with many-columned Iram, the like of which was not created in the lands."

**[1.05]** _Quranic text_

Three short verses. A people: ʿĀd. A name: Iram. And a description: dhāt al-ʿimād. Possessing ʿimād. A word that can mean columns, or pillars, or the poles that hold up a tent.

**[1.06]** _Later tradition / legend_

From these few words grew one of the most enduring mysteries of the Arabian desert. Storytellers imagined palaces of gold. Popular retellings still speak of a city of a thousand pillars. Explorers went looking for it. And satellites photographed the sand where it might lie.

**[1.07]** _Narrative framing_

This film asks three questions. What does the text actually say? What did the earliest scholars understand by it? And what, if anything, has archaeology really found?
