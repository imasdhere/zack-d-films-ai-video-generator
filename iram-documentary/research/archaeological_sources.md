# Archaeological sources

Evidence levels follow the master brief: **5 = archaeological evidence**, **6 = geographical or scientific hypothesis**.
"Primary" means that the production read the original document. "Secondary" means that the production read a summary that cites the original. The original was not accessible from this session (paywall, or the network policy).

## A. Shisr (Shiṣr / ash-Shisur), Dhofar, Oman — the site popularly called "Ubar"

### How the excavators interpreted it

- **Zarins (1996 NOVA interview, quoted in Wikipedia):** "If you look at the classical texts and the Arab historical sources, Ubar refers to a region and a group of people, not to a specific town … It's very clear on Ptolemy's second century map of the area. It says in big letters 'Iobaritae'."
- **Blom et al. 2007:** "Ubar" was more likely a **region**, the land of the Iobaritae of Ptolemy, than a city. The decline is linked to reduced frankincense demand, falling groundwater, and the shift to sea transport (secondary summary).
- **Clapp:** called the site "a convincing match" for Ubar and linked it to Quranic Iram. Wikipedia reports that "even Clapp himself denied it later", citing Blom et al. 2007. This is **not verified** in the primary text, so the film does not use it.

### Critiques

- **H. Stewart Edgell (2004)**, "The myth of the 'lost city of the Arabian Sands'", *Proceedings of the Seminar for Arabian Studies* 34: 105–120. According to secondary summaries, Edgell argued that Ubar is essentially mythical, that Shisr was a small caravanserai, and that the Rubʿ al-Khālī has been a sand-dune desert for about 6,000 years. **The original was not read.** The film cites it only as "a published critique by geologist H. Stewart Edgell".
- **Nigel Groom (1994; 2000)** questioned the identification of Shisr with Ptolemy's "Omanum Emporium" (secondary).
- **Encyclopaedia of Islam THREE**: Peter Webb, "Iram" (2019) and Andrew Rippin, "ʿĀd". As cited by Wikipedia, the Ubar–Iram identification "is not generally accepted by scholars". Not read in the original.

### Conclusion used in the film

> Shisr is a real, excavated, UNESCO-listed caravan oasis with a fortress that partly collapsed into a sinkhole. Its excavators themselves argued that "Ubar" was a region and a people, not one lost city. The link from Shisr to Quranic Iram is a **popular identification**, not an archaeological conclusion.

## B. Wadi Ramm (Wadi Rum), southern Jordan — the northern hypothesis

|---|---|---|---|

## C. Wabar craters, Rubʿ al-Khālī, Saudi Arabia — what a search for "Ubar" actually found

| Fact | Source | Access |
|---|---|---|
| H. St John Philby reached the site on 2 February 1932 while searching for the legendary city he called Wabar. He thought it was a volcano. | Philby 1933, *Geographical Journal* 81(1), via Wikipedia | Secondary |
| L. J. Spencer of the British Museum identified the site as a meteorite impact (iron about 90%, nickel about 5%). | Spencer 1933, *Mineralogical Magazine* 23: 387–404 | Secondary |
| Thermoluminescence dating by Prescott et al. (2004) suggests the impact is **less than about 250 years old**. | Prescott et al. 2004, *Meteoritics & Planetary Science* | Secondary |
| The largest iron fragment (about 2.2 tonnes) is displayed at the National Museum of Saudi Arabia, Riyadh. | Wikipedia | Secondary |

Film use: a true story about the limits of legend. A famous search for the lost city found not ruins but meteorite craters, and those craters are geologically young.

## F. What archaeology has NOT established

- No excavation has found an inscription naming any site "Iram dhāt al-ʿimād" in South Arabia.
- No destruction layer anywhere has been shown to correspond to the Quranic account of ʿĀd.
- No date for ʿĀd has been established archaeologically.
- The historicity of ʿĀd as a specific people was described as "an unanswered question" (as of 2012, per Wikipedia citing EI2). The 2025 epigraphic work suggests that a tribe named ʿĀd is attested in pre-Islamic inscriptions in the north. Whether that tribe is the Quranic ʿĀd is a scholarly proposal.
