# Geographical and scientific evidence

## 1. The Rubʿ al-Khālī (the Empty Quarter)

| Fact | Source | Access |
|---|---|---|
| Area of about 650,000 km², in parts of Saudi Arabia, Oman, the UAE and Yemen. Often called the largest continuous sand desert (sand sea) on Earth. | Wikipedia "Rubʿ al Khali" | Secondary |
| Dunes up to about 250 m high, with gravel and gypsum plains between them. | Same | Secondary |

Narration wording: "some six hundred and fifty thousand square kilometres". "Dunes that can rise more than two hundred metres."

## 2. Al-Aḥqāf (Quran 46:21)

- The Quran places Hūd's warning "at al-Aḥqāf". Pickthall: "among the wind-curved sandhills".
- Al-Ṭabarī: *aḥqāf* is the plural of *ḥiqf*, "sand that curves and bends". He identifies the land of ʿĀd as "al-Shiḥr, of the lands of Ḥaḍramawt, and what adjoins it". This is a classical interpretation (evidence level 3), primary text archived.
- Ibn Zayd (via al-Ṭabarī) speaks of "al-Aḥqāf in Ḥaḍramawt … sands like mountains".
- Modern map treatment: the map shows a **broad, soft-edged zone** labelled "al-Aḥqāf — location according to classical exegetes (Ḥaḍramawt / al-Shiḥr)". It shows no point marker and no "Iram" pin.

## 3. Palaeoclimate: a once-greener Arabia

| Finding | Source (peer-reviewed) | Access |
|---|---|---|
| A uranium-thorium-dated stalagmite from **Qunf Cave, southern Oman (Dhofar)** records monsoon rainfall from 10.3–2.7 and 1.4–0.4 thousand years before present. **After about 8,000 years ago, monsoon precipitation decreases gradually** in response to changing summer solar insolation. | Fleitmann, D., Burns, S. J., Mudelsee, M., Neff, U., Kramers, J., Mangini, A., Matter, A. (2003). *Science* 300(5626): 1737–1739. doi:10.1126/science.1083130 | Primary abstract (PubMed PMID 12805545) |
| During the peak of the Holocene Humid Period, or before it, intense rainfall in the Rubʿ al-Khālī filled a ~1,100 km² depression with a lake, which breached and carved a ~150 km-long valley. | Zaki, A. S., et al. (2025). "Monsoonal imprint on late Quaternary landscapes of the Rub' al Khali Desert." *Communications Earth & Environment* 6: 255. doi:10.1038/s43247-025-02224-1 | Primary abstract (PubMed PMID 40191807) |
| Precipitation in Dhofar is brought by the monsoon. Rock art in Arabia depicts wildlife of the humid period. | Wikipedia "African humid period" (with many citations) | Secondary |

**Editorial rule:** the humid period ended thousands of years before any historical frankincense trade. The film presents it **only** as environmental context: the land changed, and deserts were not always deserts. The film **never** links it to the destruction of ʿĀd. No study has dated ʿĀd, and the Quranic account describes a wind, not gradual drying.

## 4. Frankincense and the incense routes

| Fact | Source | Access |
|---|---|---|
| *Boswellia sacra* grows in Dhofar. Frankincense is still harvested in Wadi Dawkah. | UNESCO 1010 | Primary |
| *Periplus* §29: the Frankincense Country, "mountainous and forbidding, wrapped in thick clouds and fog, and yielding frankincense from the trees … they bear the frankincense sticking in drops on the bark". | Schoff (1912), *The Periplus of the Erythraean Sea* (public domain) | Primary |
| *Periplus* §32: "a port established for receiving the Sachalitic frankincense; the harbor is called Moscha". The frankincense "lies in heaps all over the Sachalitic country, open and unguarded … neither openly nor by stealth can it be loaded on board ship without the King's permission". | Schoff (1912) | Primary |
| Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* 12.30 (Bostock & Riley translation, public domain): "The chief productions of Arabia are frankincense and myrrh … There is no country in the world that produces frankincense except Arabia, and, indeed, not the whole of that." | Perseus Digital Library | Primary |
| Ptolemy's *Geography* names a people "Iobaritae" in Arabia Felix. | Zarins (NOVA 1996) and Blom et al. 2007, as cited | Secondary. The Greek text was not checked. |

Note: Pliny's "no country … except Arabia" is a 1st-century claim. It is factually incomplete, because frankincense also grows in the Horn of Africa, as the *Periplus* itself shows (§8–12, "far-side" frankincense). The film quotes Pliny as a voice of his time, not as a fact.
