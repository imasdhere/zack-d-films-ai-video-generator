# Controversies and uncertainties

| # | Question | Positions | Status | How the film handles it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What is "Iram"?** | A city (Alexandria per al-Quraẓī; Damascus per al-Maqburī, Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyab, ʿIkrima); a tribe or clan of ʿĀd (Qatāda; al-Ṭabarī's preference); an ancestor (Ibn Isḥāq); "the ancient" (Mujāhid); "the perished" (Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk) | Disputed since the early centuries of Islam | All views are stated, with attribution. No view is presented as fact. |
| 2 | **What is *dhāt al-ʿimād*?** | Tent-poles of nomads (Mujāhid, Qatāda; preferred by al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr); tall stature (Ibn ʿAbbās); stone columns or a building (Ibn Zayd); strength (al-Ḍaḥḥāk) | Disputed. The leading classical exegetes favour tent-poles. | This is the central twist of Chapter 3. The famous "pillars" may be the poles of tents. |
| 3 | **Is Shisr "Ubar", and is Ubar "Iram"?** | Clapp: Ubar, linked to Iram. Zarins: "Ubar" was a region or people (Iobaritae). Edgell 2004: Ubar is a myth; Shisr was a small caravanserai. Scholarly encyclopaedias (EI3): the identification is not generally accepted. | The Shisr site is real. The identification is a popular hypothesis that most scholars reject. | Chapter 4 presents the excitement of 1992 and then the scholarly reassessment. |
| 4 | **Where were ʿĀd?** | South Arabia, al-Aḥqāf in Ḥaḍramawt (classical exegetes); North Arabia and Wadi Ramm in Jordan (Al-Jallad 2025, based on inscriptions) | Open. The northern proposal is recent and rests on epigraphy. | Chapter 6 presents both, with the evidence for each. Neither is shown as solved. |
| 5 | **Did a "city of gold" exist?** | The Shaddād and Ibn Qilāba stories | Rejected by Ibn Kathīr as fables (*Israʾīliyyāt*) with unsound chains | Presented as legend, explicitly labelled. |
| 6 | **When did ʿĀd live?** | No date in the Quran. The tradition places them after Noah (Q 7:69). | Unknown | The film says so. |
| 7 | **Was the destruction a natural event?** | Some modern writers have speculated about sandstorms or a sinkhole collapse (Shisr). | No evidence links any physical event to the Quranic account. Blom et al. (2007, secondary): "no evidence that the city had perished in a sandstorm". | The film keeps the religious account and science apart, in separate sequences and with different visual grammar. |
| 8 | **Did Lawrence of Arabia coin "Atlantis of the Sands"?** | Popular attribution | Not found in his published works. Bertram Thomas used the phrase. | Correct attribution to Thomas. |
| 9 | **Did the Ebla tablets mention Iram?** | A claim in popular literature | **Unverified.** No source checked in this session supports it. | **Excluded.** |
| 10 | **Ibn Khaldūn's critique of the Iram story** | Widely reported | Primary text not retrieved in this session | **Excluded** from the narration. Flagged for a future check. |
| 11 | **Dates for Shisr** | JPL captions: "about 2800 BC to about 300 AD". Clapp: destruction AD 300–500 after an earthquake. UNESCO: "medieval fortified settlements". | Conflicting. Press-release dates are not excavation reports. | The film says only "a fortress more than two thousand years old in its earliest phases, according to its excavators" and "reused into the Islamic period". |

## Known gaps in this research (honest limitations)

- Several archaeological details come from secondary summaries (Wikipedia) of primary works: Blom et al. 2007, Zarins 1997 and 2001, and Edgell 2004. The originals are behind paywalls or blocked hosts. Each such claim is marked "Secondary" in the claim matrix and worded cautiously.
- The Al-Jallad 2025 article was read through its abstract and secondary summaries only.
- The tafsir texts come from a digital copy (quran.com API) whose printed edition is not identified, so no volume or page numbers are given.
