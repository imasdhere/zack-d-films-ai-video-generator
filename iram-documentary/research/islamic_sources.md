# Islamic sources: tafsir, hadith and later traditions

All Arabic quotations below are **verbatim** copies of the archived source files: `raw_sources/tafsir/` (in git) and the sunnah.com page captures (kept outside git for copyright; see `raw_sources/README.md`). The English glosses are the production team's own working translations, marked **[gloss]**. Do not present them on screen as published translations.

## 1. Tafsir al-Ṭabarī — *Jāmiʿ al-Bayān ʿan Taʾwīl Āy al-Qurʾān*

- Author: Abū Jaʿfar Muḥammad ibn Jarīr al-Ṭabarī (d. 310 AH / 923 CE).
- Text used: quran.com API, tafsir resource id 15 ("الطبري"), entries 89:6, 89:7 and 89:8. Retrieved 2026-11-19 from `https://api.quran.com/api/v4/tafsirs/15/by_ayah/89:7`.
- Edition: not identified by the source. Printed page numbers are **not verified**. Cite by verse (Q 89:6–8).

### Opinions he reports on *Iram* (89:7)

| View | Attribution in al-Ṭabarī |
|---|---|
| The name of a town: Alexandria | al-Quraẓī (Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb) |
| The name of a town: Damascus | al-Maqburī |
| A nation (*umma*) | Mujāhid |
| "The ancient one" | Mujāhid (another chain) |
| A tribe of ʿĀd, "the royal house of ʿĀd" | Qatāda |
| A tribe of ʿĀd called Iram, the grandfather of ʿĀd | Qatāda (via Maʿmar) |
| Lineage: ʿĀd ibn Iram ibn ʿAwṣ ibn Sām ibn Nūḥ | Ibn Isḥāq |
| "The perished" (from *arama*, to destroy) | Ibn ʿAbbās (via the ʿAwfī chain); al-Ḍaḥḥāk |

**His own preferred view (verbatim):**

> وأشبه الأقوال فيه بالصواب عندي أنها اسم قبيلة من عاد

[gloss] "The most likely of these views to be correct, in my opinion, is that it is the name of a tribe of ʿĀd." His reason is grammatical: the word is not inflected as a construct and takes no tanwīn.

### Opinions he reports on *dhāt al-ʿimād*

| View | Attribution |
|---|---|
| Tall stature ("their height was like columns") | Ibn ʿAbbās (ʿAwfī chain); Mujāhid |
| People of tent-poles: nomads who moved to follow the rains and pasture | Mujāhid; Qatāda |
| A building they built, with raised pillars, at al-Aḥqāf in Ḥaḍramawt | Ibn Zayd |
| Strength and power | al-Ḍaḥḥāk |

**His own preferred view (verbatim):**

> وأشبه الأقوال في ذلك بما دلّ عليه ظاهر التنـزيل قول من قال: عُنِيَ بذلك أنهم كانوا أهل عمود، سيارة لأن المعروف في كلام العرب من العماد، ما عمل به الخيام من الخشب والسواري التي يحمل عليها البناء، ولا يعلم بناء كان لهم بالعماد بخبر صحيح

[gloss] "The view that best fits the apparent sense of the revelation is that of those who said it means they were people of poles: travellers (nomads). In the speech of the Arabs, *ʿimād* commonly means the wooden poles used for tents and the columns that carry a building. **And no building of theirs with columns is known through any authentic report.**"

> فأما عماد البنيان، فلا يعلم كثير أحد من أهل التأويل وجهه إليه

[gloss] "As for building-columns, hardly anyone among the exegetes is known to have taken it that way."

### On 89:8 and on where ʿĀd lived (verbatim)

> فإن بلاد عاد هي التي وصفها الله في كتابه فقال: وَاذْكُرْ أَخَا عَادٍ إِذْ أَنْذَرَ قَوْمَهُ بِالأَحْقَافِ والأحقاف: هي جمع حِقْف، وهو ما انعطف من الرمل وانحنى، وليست الإسكندرية ولا دمشق من بلاد الرمال، بل ذلك الشِّحْرَ من بلاد حضرموت، وما والاها.

[gloss] "The land of ʿĀd is the one God described in His Book: 'Remember the brother of ʿĀd when he warned his people at al-Aḥqāf.' *Al-Aḥqāf* is the plural of *ḥiqf*: sand that curves and bends. Neither Alexandria nor Damascus is a land of sands. Rather it is al-Shiḥr, of the lands of Ḥaḍramawt, and what adjoins it."

He also reports Qatāda's statement that "they were twelve cubits tall". This is a report of an early opinion, not an established fact. **The film does not use it.**

## 2. Tafsir Ibn Kathīr — *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm*

- Author: ʿImād al-Dīn Ismāʿīl ibn ʿUmar ibn Kathīr (d. 774 AH / 1373 CE).
- Text used: quran.com API, tafsir resource id 14 ("ابن كثير"), entries 89:6–8. Retrieved 2026-11-19. Edition not identified by the source. Cite by verse.
- English abridgement (Darussalam, resource id 169) was downloaded for reference only. It is copyrighted and **not quoted**.

### Key statements (verbatim)

Lineage (89:6):
> وهؤلاء عاد الأولى ، وهم أولاد عاد بن إرم بن عوص بن سام بن نوح ، قاله ابن إسحاق

[gloss] "These are the first ʿĀd, the descendants of ʿĀd son of Iram son of ʿAwṣ son of Sām son of Nūḥ — so said Ibn Isḥāq."

Meaning of *dhāt al-ʿimād* (89:7):
> ( ذات العماد ) لأنهم كانوا يسكنون بيوت الشعر التي ترفع بالأعمدة الشداد

[gloss] "'Possessing *ʿimād*', because they lived in tents of hair raised upon strong poles."

He notes that Ibn Jarīr (al-Ṭabarī) chose the tent-pole view and rejected the "tall stature" view, and agrees: «واختار الأول ابن جرير ، ورد الثاني فأصاب» [gloss: "Ibn Jarīr chose the first and rejected the second — and he was right."]

Summary judgement (89:8):
> فعلى كل قول سواء كانت العماد أبنية بنوها ، أو أعمدة بيوتهم للبدو ، أو سلاحا يقاتلون به ، أو طول الواحد منهم - فهم قبيلة وأمة من الأمم

[gloss] "On every view — whether the *ʿimād* were buildings they built, the poles of Bedouin dwellings, weapons they fought with, or the height of each of them — they were a tribe, one nation among the nations."

On the city stories:
> ومن زعم أن المراد بقوله : ( إرم ذات العماد ) مدينة إما دمشق ... أو إسكندرية ... ففيه نظر

[gloss] "Whoever claims that Iram dhāt al-ʿimād means a city — whether Damascus … or Alexandria … — that is open to objection."

> من ذكر مدينة يقال لها : ( إرم ذات العماد ) مبنية بلبن الذهب والفضة ... وأنها تنتقل فتارة تكون بأرض الشام ، وتارة باليمن ، وتارة بالعراق ... فإن هذا كله من خرافات الإسرائيليين ، من وضع بعض زنادقتهم

[gloss] "… mention of a city called Iram dhāt al-ʿimād built of bricks of gold and silver … and that it moves about, sometimes in Syria, sometimes in Yemen, sometimes in Iraq … all of this is among the fables of the Israʾīliyyāt, fabricated by some of their heretics."

On the story of ʿAbd Allāh ibn Qilāba (a Bedouin who, in the time of Muʿāwiya, was said to have found the city while looking for lost camels; transmitted by al-Thaʿlabī and others):
> فهذه الحكاية ليس يصح إسنادها ... وهذا مما يقطع بعدم صحته

[gloss] "This tale does not have a sound chain of transmission … and it is among the things that are certainly not authentic."

### Narrations that Ibn Kathīr mentions and the film will NOT use

| Narration | Why it is excluded |
|---|---|
| Via Ibn Abī Ḥātim, from al-Miqdām, from the Prophet ﷺ: a man of Iram would lift a rock and drop it on a settlement | The chain contains «عمن حدثه» ("from someone who told him"): an unnamed narrator. Weak. No grading is claimed here. |
| Thawr ibn Zayd reports reading a written text: "I am Shaddād ibn ʿĀd, I raised the pillars …" | A report of reading an unnamed book. Not a hadith. Legendary. |
| The gold-and-silver city / moving city | Rejected by Ibn Kathīr himself as Israʾīliyyāt (see above). |

A sunnah.com search for "Iram" returned **no matching hadith** (archived search page). The film does not state that any authentic hadith describes Iram.

The film assigns no authenticity grades beyond the collections' own status: both collections are *Ṣaḥīḥ*, and the hadiths above are in their main text.

## 4. Later traditions (evidence level 4)

| Tradition | Source status | Film treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Shaddād ibn ʿĀd built Iram as an earthly paradise | Later narrative literature (qiṣaṣ, al-Thaʿlabī). Rejected by Ibn Kathīr. *1001 Nights* nights 277–279 (Burton, "The City of Many-Columned Iram and Abdullah son of Abi Kilabah"), per Wikipedia. | Presented as legend, clearly labelled. |
| ʿAbd Allāh ibn Qilāba found the city in Muʿāwiya's time | Chain judged unsound by Ibn Kathīr. | Presented as a story that scholars themselves doubted. |
| Tomb of the Prophet Hūd (Qabr Hūd) in Ḥaḍramawt, a pilgrimage site | A living tradition, reported in EI2 "Hūd" (Wensinck & Pellat) as cited by Wikipedia. Several sites are claimed. | Mentioned as tradition only. No depiction. |
| Ibn Khaldūn's critique of the Iram city story (al-Muqaddimah) | **Not verified.** The full text could not be retrieved in this session. | **Excluded** from the narration. |

## 5. Editorial rules that follow from the sources

1. **No depiction of the Prophet Hūd** in any form: face, figure or silhouette. He is referred to in narration only. This follows the widely held Islamic convention against depicting prophets.
2. The people of ʿĀd may appear only as distant, anonymous figures, small in a landscape. No individual is identified.
3. The film does not dramatise the punishment with bodies. The wind is shown as landscape and weather only.
4. Quranic text appears on screen in Arabic (Tanzil, unaltered) with the Pickthall translation and a verse reference.
5. "City of a thousand pillars" is treated as a later image, not a Quranic description. The Quran uses *dhāt al-ʿimād*; it gives no number.
